## Outcome

**Changes Required** (recommended).

The work splits in two:

- **Policy exceptions (#605).** These are well built on the GOV-001 registry. Someone asks for one, a platform admin approves it, it's recorded in `catalog.policy_exception`, the audit and the policy's history, and it ends on its date. Check now and Explain show it.
- **Project exceptions in `exceptions.toml` (#603).** FD-129's criteria are written about these, and they are now shown with a notice before they end. They are still lines someone types into a project file. There's no request, no approval and no audit record of who asked or who approved.

Three gaps were found. Two are fixed in #838 (quality PASS 9.0 at 3eca40dd): policy exceptions now warn 14 days before they end, and a platform admin can end one early with a reason. One remains: FD-129's third criterion isn't met for the file it names. That needs code or a product decision: the owner could narrow FD-129 so that policy exceptions are the governed path and `exceptions.toml` stays a developer's file.

Recommended by: Engineering quality review. Decided by (product owner): pending.

## Where the criteria come from

PB-059's criterion is "As defined in FD-129". I found FD-129 in docs/product/enterprise-backlog.md:126–129:

> exceptions in `exceptions.toml` show in the app with owner, reason, approver and expiry; expiring ones raise a notice; adding one records who asked and who approved in the audit trail (FD-125).

Line 129 (added by #603) still says: "Still open: asking for an exception in the app, and recording who asked and who approved in the audit trail." GOV-001 is the policy registry in docs/governance.md. PB-059's own capability line is "Request, approve and expire exceptions with history, aligned with the policy registry." I reviewed against both.
